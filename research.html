Maneet Mehta
experience research

My research so far sits where machine learning meets its social consequences: measuring bias in generative models, testing whether language models explain themselves faithfully, and studying how institutions should decide which decisions to hand to automated systems.

Papers & Posters

  • Generating Fearful Images: Emotional Biases in Image-Generation Models
    First author · arXiv:2411.05985 · with Cody Buntain, University of Maryland

    Quantifies systematic emotional bias in text-to-image models. Per-emotion true- and false-positive rates over 10,000 prompt–image pairs, with bootstrapped 95% confidence intervals, across Stable Diffusion, SDXL, and GPT-Image-1.5. Emotion classifiers were fine-tuned ViT, Swin, and ConvNeXT models trained on 118k images and validated against human review.

  • Breaking into the Playground of Privilege
    Sole author · Journal of Student Research 13(3) · 2024

    A literature review and expert interviews on why nomination-based screening, biased testing, and socioeconomic barriers under-identify minority students for Gifted and Talented programs, with recommendations for universal screening and local norming.

  • LockdownGPT: Assessing Adversarial Attacks on LLMs
    Poster · Johns Hopkins Applied Physics Laboratory · 2025 · internal, available on request

    A demonstration of adversarial attacks on LLMs, covering three jailbreak classes and twenty red-team prompts against frontier guardrails. Presented to government officials including the former Deputy CTO of the White House Office of Science and Technology Policy.

In Progress

  • Faithfulness of LLM self-explanations
    Supervised Program for Alignment Research · Sep 2026 –

    Four controlled experiments on counterfactual simulatability techniques, showing that a widely used metric is confounded by up to 31 percentage points of inference-pipeline noise. I built the multi-provider evaluation harness, roughly 25,000 calls across five models, and led the midterm report.

  • When should institutions delegate decisions to AI?
    Centre for Digital Governance, Hertie School · with Prof. Joanna J. Bryson · manuscript in preparation

    A decision framework for delegation to AI given the cost of human oversight, grounded in an LLM-assisted classification of 3,700+ government AI deployments from U.S., German, and Dutch public inventories.
