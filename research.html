Research

My research so far sits where machine learning meets its social consequences: measuring bias in generative models, probing how language models fail under adversarial pressure, and studying how public institutions should decide which tasks to hand to automated systems.

Papers & Posters

  • Assessing Emotions in Images and Potential Biases in Generative Models
    First author · arXiv:2411.05985 · with Cody Buntain (University of Maryland) · 2025

    Formalizes a TPR/FPR-based statistical framework for measuring emotion bias in text-to-image models. Analyzing roughly 10,000 prompt–image pairs, the paper finds a consistent and robust bias toward fear in generated images, validated across model architectures and against human annotation. I designed the annotation and analysis pipeline and fine-tuned vision transformer classifiers for emotion recognition.

  • LockdownGPT: Assessing Adversarial Attacks on LLMs
    Poster · Johns Hopkins University Applied Physics Laboratory Showcase · 2025 · internal publication, available on request

    A technical demonstration of systemic vulnerabilities that large language models face across their development lifecycle. The project organized jailbreak classes, authored red-team prompts, and stress-tested common guardrails against prompt injection, role confusion, and refusal circumvention. Presented to government officials including the former Deputy CTO of the White House Office of Science and Technology Policy.

In Progress

  • Human oversight burdens and AI task allocation in public administration
    Centre for Digital Governance, Hertie School · Prof. Joanna J. Bryson's group · Jul. 2026 – present

    A conceptual and empirical study of how public-sector administrative processes should divide work between officials and automated systems, and what meaningful human oversight costs in practice. I lead the project and am first-authoring the resulting paper with Chris Schmitz and a colleague.

Earlier Work

  • NLP pipeline for U.S. defense posture analysis
    German Council on Foreign Relations / Yale Foreign Policy Initiative · 2025 – 2026

    Designed and built a multi-stage hybrid pipeline (semantic filtering, LLM-based structured extraction, HDBSCAN clustering, and narrative synthesis) to process NDAAs, National Security Strategies, and National Defense Strategies and track shifts in U.S. force-posture commitments to European defense. A related memo on the Strategic Posture Commission report is in Notes.

  • Computer vision for autonomous following
    Johns Hopkins Applied Physics Laboratory · 2023 – 2025

    Built a full-pipeline neural-network image classifier and a computer vision program that allowed robots to autonomously follow target items.
